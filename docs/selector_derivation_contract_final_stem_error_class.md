CausalFeature: a direction-bearing note or chord belongs to a complete, resolved printed rhythmic unit, which can be a single onset or a primary beam with all of its attached chord heads
CausalFeature: the final simultaneous sounding occupancy on that staff resolves the unit as isolated or as one of two rhythmically independent voice roles
CausalFeature: for an isolated unit with visible heads on only one side of a known middle staff line, the head position determines the preferred direction
CausalFeature: simultaneous independent voices with strictly ordered visible heads and stable relative roles require opposite stem directions
CausalFeature: at least one plain explicit stem in the complete unit conflicts with the direction established by its final printed context
NonCausalContextExcluded: piece, filename, measure label, page, system number, source identifier, original voice label value, corpus frequency and discovery order

The complete rhythmic unit is the scope of the direction. An intermediate
standalone note can become part of a primary beam. The final serialized
membership and voice occupancy therefore determine the observed defect.

The definition leaves ambiguous cases open. These include overlapping or
crossing roles, a beam with unknown members, and uncertain printed pitch due
to a clef or octave transition. An explicitly cross-staff beam can have its
own direction rules. Neither a visible difference from a reference PDF nor
the absence of a competing stem by itself establishes this class.

This document defines the error class. It adds no classifier, thresholds,
mutation or new writer. General rules must survive relabeling and independent
duplication. Corpus distribution appears only in later evaluation.
