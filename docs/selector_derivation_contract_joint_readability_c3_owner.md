## Unchanged policy and closed scope

The unchanged accepted `joint_register_dp_policy_v1.md` owns the C3 objective,
head protection, incumbent retention, tie policy and completeness obligations.
This contract introduces no new weight, threshold or corpus-triggered condition.
Numerical corpus evaluation targets are acceptance observations, not selectors.

The original register creator remains responsible for octave directions and
their origins. The original clef materializers remain responsible for clefs.
The joint finite search is an internal component of those existing owners;
it grants no new filesystem sink, writer family or alternative repair pass.
Upper-domain clef alternatives require existing materialization capability;
the existence of a nominal state in the search kernel is not that capability.

Carried source/attack identity is not recreated by XML similarity, document
order or a unique coordinate match. Field checks validate existing locators.
Positive and continuation fragment roles must be bound to the retained attack.
Missing or inconsistent identity, group, time, spelling or authority evidence
keeps the complete incumbent and is reported as UNKNOWN.

Group information must come from the executed original producer and must bind
the shared source document. A capture getter that has no producer execution yet
is not evidence of an empty group set. Existing actual producer receipts and
representation transports continue to own subsequent custody and final artifact
identity. Render membership is a separate obligation: a Master capture alone
does not prove the final Render groups. Quarter-note boundaries and corpus group
distributions grant no permission to ignore that obligation.

The shared boundary follows the executed E053/IR10 owners and precedes the
cursor check, export snapshot, one-shot late Master epoch and final sounding
material freeze. A completed frozen voice-label plan is not an input at that
boundary. Concrete original material bindings, actual producer groups and
their recorded continuations are required instead. The early register session
cannot be rebound merely by changing its document hash.
Any replacement of already materialized octave directions must preserve their
original custody and must use the existing register writer. Separating that
writer from plan selection does not itself authorize replacement or adoption.

The original early course cannot be omitted on the assumption that downstream
readers ignore it. Existing clef planning observes the octave display. Keeping
that early path therefore remains the incumbent until an equivalent complete
pipeline continuation is proved. This is an owner-dependency constraint, not a
corpus-derived exception.

Origin-registry preparation operates on a complete copy of the reader session.
Replacement requires confirmed original births and complete original marker
coverage, and is unavailable after downstream probes, plans or claims exist.
The proposed complete marker set must validate before it can replace the active
set. A changed reader session refuses commit without overwriting intervening
observations. Unregistered and ambiguous creator markers are errors. These
registry properties alone do not establish atomic XML/lineage adoption: that
must be closed separately at the existing productive owner before C3 activation.

The existing octave BLOCK journal continues to bind the actual owner receipt
and original measure span. A nonempty removal additionally requires one whole
direction with a unique confirmed creator origin from the active reader session,
matching original part, staff, display, kind and line. Only octave markup and
its original continuation cue may be removed; foreign words, dynamics, sound,
rhythmic content, ambiguous markers and downstream reader observations refuse
the operation. Removal and insertion are separate recorded operations. This
adds no receipt mint or writer: the unchanged existing octave authority must
still execute and authenticate the actual transition.

Later independent clef decisions cannot silently replace an adopted joint
course. Existing owners must either materialize the selected bound course or
preserve it with a successful final state/cost/head-protection check. A course
state readback alone is not a proof of source or fragment preservation.

Independent physical domains are optimized independently only after their
dependencies close. Unsupported cross-domain coupling remains UNKNOWN. A
greedy sequence of mutually changing boundary contexts is not a joint optimum;
local contextual costs are never blindly added into a global acceptance total.

and anchors, complete incumbent representation, retained source identities,

tied fragments, source renaming/relocation, independent duplication, unsupported
